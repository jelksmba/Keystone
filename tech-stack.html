One platform instead of twenty vendors

Lenders run their loan origination system, then buy a second layer of tools around it: verifications, income calculators, ordering portals, compliance engines, QC software, CRMs. This page maps that outside stack and decides, item by item, how Keystone brings it inside.

Three ways to bring a tool inside

Think of Keystone like a phone. The phone maker designs the experience and the apps, but buys the camera sensor and the cell modem from specialists. Customers still feel like they bought one product.

Build

We own it

Our own code and logic. Where Keystone wins: the analysis, the workflow, the automation, the experience.

Embed

Inside, under our screen

A data partner powers it, but the lender never leaves Keystone and gets one contract and one bill. Usually data that only a specialist can supply.

Integrate

Connect what they already use

Required systems (Fannie, Freddie) or heavy legal and capital products where the lender has a vendor already. We connect to it cleanly.

What we build ourselves, and why

  • Income, asset, credit and property analysis. This is the heart of underwriting and your strongest experience. Owning the calculations means the loan, the documents and the decision all live in one place. Standalone income calculators exist because LOSs never did this well.
  • Document classification and data extraction on top of an OCR engine. We rent the "eyes" (OCR) and build the "brain": what the document is, what it should match, and what to do when it doesn't.
  • Ordering hub. One place, triggered by milestones, that orders and tracks every third-party item. The vendors behind it can change without the user noticing.
  • 100% QC and the stated-versus-verified engine. Today a separate QC system samples 10–20% of loans after the fact. Ours checks every loan, at every step.
  • Workflow, conditions, alerts, reporting, leaderboards. This replaces task tools, robotic process automation bots and BI dashboards lenders buy separately.
  • Point of sale and a lightweight CRM. The borrower's front door and the loan officer's follow-up list belong in the same system as the loan.

What we do not build, and why

  • Automated underwriting (DU, LPA, GUS). Required by the agencies. Integrate.
  • Pricing engine. Lenders' rate sheets and investor relationships already live there. Integrate.
  • Credit bureau data. Licensed, regulated (FCRA). Embed through a credit reseller.
  • Closing document generation. The legal liability for state-by-state forms is the product. Integrate the lender's doc vendor.
  • eNote vault, MERS, remote notary. Specialized, certified infrastructure. Integrate.
  • Hedging and loan sale. Capital-markets desks have deep, specialized tools. Integrate in a later phase.

How the business model works

  • One contract, one bill. Platform fee per closed loan, with data costs (credit, verifications, appraisals) passed through on the same invoice. This replaces many vendor contracts and invoices.
  • Verification waterfall. Try the cheapest verified source first (borrower-permissioned payroll and bank data), and only fall back to more expensive databases when that fails. The savings are a selling point.
  • Swap vendors without the lender noticing. Because orders run through our hub, we can change or add data partners for price or quality.
  • Vendor management made easy. Lenders must review every third-party vendor. One vendor (us) with strong security reviews (SOC 2), and partners already approved, shortens that work.

Watch-outs

  • Data costs are real. Verification and credit fees are per pull. Price the bundle so heavy-use lenders don't erase the margin.
  • Regulated data. Credit (FCRA), consumer financial data (GLBA), and borrower consent for every pull. Build consent and audit logging in from day one.
  • "All in one" has to be as good as best-of-breed. Lenders will compare our income calculation to dedicated tools. Lead with the pieces where we are clearly better.
  • Lenders with existing contracts. Some will keep their pricing engine or CRM for a while. Every embedded tool should also be able to run in "connect your own" mode.
