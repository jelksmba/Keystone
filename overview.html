The four C's of underwriting

Underwriting is organized around the four C's, with an analysis screen for each. Rules adjust to the loan program: Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, FHA, VA, USDA or Non-QM.

CScreenWhat it does
CharacterCredit analysisReconciles the credit report with the application; flags missing or mismatched debts; applies program rules for payments not reported (for example, revolving and student loans)
CapacityIncome and employment analysisCalculates qualifying income from verified payroll and tax data; shows the calculation step by step; tracks verbal verification timing
CapitalAsset analysisFunds to close, reserves, gifts, seller credits, large deposits; spots recurring payments on bank statements that may be undisclosed debts
CollateralProperty analysisValue from the AVM and the appraisal with confidence; property type, taxes, insurance and HOA checked against records; appraisal review checklist

A 1008-style summary pulls the four together with a recommendation and compensating factors. Automated underwriting results from DU, LPA and GUS are shown in the file.

Alerts and conditions

Alerts close the gaps between what the application says and what was verified. Each alert shows the application value, the verified value and its source, with one click to update the application. Every update is logged.

Alerts cover

  • Missing information on the application, such as a former address found on credit
  • Payment amounts corrected from the credit report or program rules
  • Liabilities found on credit or bank statements but missing from the application
  • Income updated to the verified income calculation
  • Asset balances updated from statements
  • Housing costs updated from tax records and insurance quotes

The pipeline shows an alert count for each loan, and the loan file shows it at the top.

Conditions across the whole loan

Conditions are generated by rules as the loan moves. The underwriter validates them before they reach the borrower. Each has a person assigned to do the work and a role that signs it off.

Compliance and quality control

Compliance and QC checks run automatically at every milestone change, on every loan, and users see each result.

  • Disclosure timing. The TRID clock starts when the sixth application item arrives. Keystone tracks Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure deadlines using the correct business-day rules, including federal holidays, and flags changes that restart the waiting period.
  • Fee tolerances. Zero and 10% tolerance buckets are checked against the closing agent's figures before closing.
  • Rules covered. TRID, RESPA, ability-to-repay and QM, HOEPA and higher-priced loan tests, ECOA appraisal delivery, loan officer compensation, HMDA data, identity and OFAC screening, and rate lock coverage.
  • 100% QC. Every loan is checked for data mismatches, undisclosed debts, DTI drift and fraud alerts, instead of a sample after closing. Formal post-closing QC programs still apply; Keystone makes them easier.
  • Audit trail. Every change records who made it, when, and the value before and after: application edits, documents, conditions, pricing, fees and milestone moves.

Closing audit, post-close and investor delivery

No wire is requested until the signed closing package has been audited against the loan file.

Before funding

Closing audit

When the signed package comes back, Keystone reads each document and compares it with the loan:

  • Borrower name, property address, loan amount, rate, payment, first payment date and closing date
  • Cash to close against the final settlement statement, and APR on the final Closing Disclosure
  • Every required document present, signed and dated

Each finding is marked corrected, requested from the closing agent (adds a prior-to-funding condition), or accepted with a note. Funding unlocks when no blocking issue remains.

After funding

Post-close audit

A second audit confirms the funded loan matches the package and tracks trailing documents such as the recorded deed of trust and final title policy, MERS registration and HMDA data.

To the investor

Investor delivery

Keystone builds the package from the eFolder in the investor's stacking order, with the loan data file (MISMO 3.4). Agency loans go through Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac delivery systems, with the original note to the document custodian. Other investors receive files through their portal or secure transfer, never plain email. The lock desk then reconciles the purchase advice and pays off the warehouse line.

Switching without disruption

Changing an LOS is a big decision, so Keystone makes the move gradual and supported.

  1. Bring your loans over

    In-progress and past loans are imported from your current LOS, documents included, using the industry-standard MISMO format.

  2. Run both side by side

    New applications start in Keystone while loans already in process finish where they started. Reporting covers both.

  3. Set up for you

    Fee tables, conditions, workflows, users and file contacts are configured from how your team works today.

  4. Role-based training

    Short onboarding for each role, reinforced by the built-in workflow checklists.

  5. Agreed success targets

    A go-live checklist and 90-day measures such as time to clear to close and data gaps per file.

  6. Contract transition help

    Credits toward the remaining term of the system you are leaving.
